How to study

Use the same four steps for every lesson

1. Start with the idea

Read the short explanation first. Do not begin by memorising every detail.

2. Understand the mechanism

Ask what changes, which molecule or enzyme is involved, and why the step matters.

3. Check the common mistake

Use the misconception box to separate similar terms and avoid typical exam errors.

4. Test yourself

Close the lesson, recall the idea in your own words, then try a question or flashcard.

Must-know equations

Keep these four relationships ready

pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])Relates buffer pH to conjugate-base/acid ratio under stated assumptions.
v = Vmax[S]/(Km + [S])Describes initial velocity for the Michaelis–Menten model.
ΔG = ΔG°′ + RT ln QConnects biochemical standard free energy with actual cellular conditions.
ΔG°′ = −nFΔE°′Relates redox-potential difference to standard free-energy change.

    How to build university answers

    Practical laboratory companion

    Use this as a preparation and record-writing guide. Perform wet-laboratory work only with departmental protocols, supervision, appropriate protective equipment and approved waste disposal.

    PracticalCore evidence to recordInterpretation checkpoint
    pH meter and buffer preparationCalibration standards, target pH, measured pH and temperatureExplain pKa, useful buffer range and why dilution can reduce capacity.
    Carbohydrate identificationControls and observations for reducing sugar, ketose or polysaccharide testsSeparate a chemical test result from a claim of sample purity.
    Protein estimationBlank, standards, calibration curve, dilution and unknown calculationState the chosen assay and relevant reagent interference.
    Enzyme kineticsInitial rates across substrate concentration, pH, temperature or inhibitorFit the intended model; do not infer causation from one altered point.
    Paper or thin-layer chromatographyOrigin, solvent front, spot positions, Rf values and standardsRf is condition-dependent and is supporting—not unique—identification evidence.
    Gel filtration or ion exchangeFraction number, absorbance/activity and elution profileConnect elution order to accessible size or charge under the stated buffer.
    SDS-PAGE or agarose electrophoresisLane map, marker, migration distance and documented imageExplain what molecular property the gel resolves and what it cannot prove.
    UV–visible spectrophotometryWavelength, blank, path length, absorbance and dilution factorCheck the linear range before applying Beer–Lambert calculations.
    Cell fractionationRotor, radius/RCF, duration, temperature, fractions and marker assayDescribe enrichment rather than claiming that a fraction is absolutely pure.
    Quantitative data reportReplicates, units, mean, variation, graph, sample calculation and limitationsDistinguish precision, accuracy, biological variation and technical error.
    Recommended practical-record structure
    1. Aim and testable principle
    2. Materials and supervised safety requirements
    3. Method with controls and variables
    4. Raw observations with units
    5. Calculations and graph
    6. Result stated without overclaiming
    7. Sources of error, limitations and one improvement
